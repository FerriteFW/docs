## 37. The terminal and mini CPS

### What it's for

A plain-text interface over the cable that any terminal program can use: a console, a live monitor, and a small CPS for channels, zones and settings. No web app or driver needed.

### Using it

1. Connect the programming cable and open a terminal (screen, minicom, PuTTY) at 115200 8N1, VT100.
2. Press Enter. The radio answers with a menu: **Radio · Channels · Zones · Settings · APRS · GPS · Monitor · About**.
3. Arrows move, Enter edits, Esc goes back, digits jump (the same numbers as the radio's menu).

**Cable port** (MENU 0 9 2 3): *auto* picks the mode from the first bytes received: link frames mean the web app, a typed Enter means the terminal. *Link*, *terminal* and *KISS* force one.

**Command line.** `:` on any page opens it on the bottom line; Enter runs it, Esc closes it, Up brings the last command back. The answer shows on the bottom line until the next key; `:help` lists the commands.

| Command | Does |
| --- | --- |
| `set 0 7 1 Dark` | A setting by its number in the radio's menu and a value as the radio shows it (any case) |
| `get 0 7 1`, `get` | One setting; every setting not at its default |
| `ch 12` | Channel 12 as its `ch` line |
| `ch 12 power high` | One field: `name rx offset tx mode tone rxtone txtone power zone step squelch busy reverse skip aprs rxonly hidden priority` (`offset=split tx=146.52` for a split, `squelch=global`; `bw` is still read from older dumps, VFO-20) |
| `ch 12 "Club" 443.475 offset=+5 tone=100.0` | A whole channel; fields not given get their defaults |
| `ch 12 pos=45.4215,-75.6972` | Channel 12's position (CHAN-2): degrees, north and east positive, or a grid square (FN25dk); `pos=none` clears it. The `ch` line writes it, so `dump` and `load` keep it; a whole channel line without `pos=` keeps the position it has |
| `ch 12 delete` | Deletes channel 12 |
| `zone 2 "Repeaters" scan=on` | A zone, written whole (zones are made in order) |
| `preset 1 "2 m" 144 148 step=12.5kHz` | A range preset |
| `home 12`, `home none` | The home channel |
| `set 0 6 9 45.4215,-75.6972` | The fixed position (GPS-3): degrees, a grid square, or `none` (its altitude is kept) |
| `dump`, `load` | Everything as text; read it back |
| `clear channels 21` | Deletes every channel; the number must be how many there are |

The debug and bench commands (`mem`, `gpio`, `bk`, `si`, `bt`, `rx`, `mic`, `txtest`, `gps query` and the rest, TERM-21 to TERM-36) are in the bench builds only, bring-up and `tx-bench`; the images you install say "not in this build" (TERM-38). `gps`, `slot`, `ui`, `key` and `knob` are in every build.

**Backing up: dump and load.** `:dump` prints everything as the same commands, one per line, ending with `end`; save it with the terminal program's log or capture. `:load` reads such a file back: send it (paste, or the program's send-file) and it ends at the line `end` or Ctrl-D. Each line is checked as if it were typed; a line that fails is skipped whole and named with its line number, the rest go in, and a count closes the load. Turn on XON/XOFF flow control in the terminal program, or send with a short delay per line: the radio pauses the sender while it writes to flash.

```
# Ferrite dump v1, firmware 0.1.0
# Settings, by their numbers in the radio's menu
set 0 7 1 Dark                    # Theme
set 0 6 6 24 h, GPS               # Clock
# 0 9 4 Region: Canada (changes only on the radio)
# Zones (a channel's zone=N is one of these)
zone 2 "Repeaters" scan=on shown=on distance=off resume=radio dwell=radio pri1=12
# Range presets (0 1 4)
preset 3 "Airband" 118.00000 137.00000 step=25kHz mode=AM
# Channels (21 of 4000)
ch 12 "Club repeater" 443.47500 offset=+5.00000 mode=NFM txtone=100.0 rxtone=off power=low zone=2
ch 31 "Guard" 121.50000 offset=simplex mode=AM tone=off power=low zone=4
ch 40 "Cross" 446.00000 offset=split tx=146.52000 mode=NFM tone=off power=low squelch=global
home 12
end
```

Lines are merged by number: a `ch`, `zone` or `preset` line replaces what it names and nothing else is deleted, so a load adds to a radio; `clear channels N` first for a clean copy. A channel line always carries its frequency, offset (with `tx=` for a split), mode (which is the bandwidth, VFO-20), tones and power; zone, step, squelch (`global` or 0–9), busy lock and the flags only when not the default. Region and callsign are set on the radio during setup and appear only as notes, so load into a radio that has been set up.

### Settings

| Menu | Setting | Values | Default |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

> **Spec: the terminal**
>
> - **TERM-1** VT100 at 80 × 24 over the cable; works with any terminal program, no special client.
> - **TERM-2** In *auto*, the first bytes decide: a 0x00 (the link's frame delimiter; the web app sends one before its first frame) gives the cable to the link protocol, which keeps it while frames or syncs keep coming (10 s); a typed Enter selects the terminal; KISS only when KISS mode or *KISS* is chosen. *Link* and *Terminal* fix the port to one of them; *KISS* leaves both silent (chapter 26).
> - **TERM-3** The cable is trusted (LINK-7): never asked for the PIN.
> - **TERM-4** The terminal never gets in the radio's way.
>     - **TERM-4.1** The radio keeps working while the terminal is open.
>     - **TERM-4.2** The terminal never blocks receive.
>     - **TERM-4.3** The terminal never blocks scan.
>     - **TERM-4.4** The terminal never blocks transmit.
> - **TERM-5** Monitor shows decoded packets, APRS and decoder hits as they arrive, one line each, until Esc.

> **Spec: the mini CPS**
>
> - **TERM-6** Channels, zones and settings are edited from the same settings table as the radio's menus: same names, numbers, values and validation.
> - **TERM-7** Writes follow LINK-5: frequencies, tones and power must be legal for the band; region and TX limits can't be changed.
> - **TERM-8** The calibration sector and the update area are never reachable (LINK-3).
> - **TERM-9** Changes save as on the radio (queued to the Storage task, within 2.5 s); the radio's screen updates at once.
> - **TERM-10** A change made on the radio while the terminal shows it is redrawn in the terminal.
> - **TERM-20** On connect, the terminal shows a plain text banner (no ASCII art): firmware and version, callsign and link, and "Press Enter for the menu, ? for help". `about` shows it again.
> - **TERM-21** Debug commands, for bring-up and fault-finding without a custom build (all but `gps` alone are the bench builds', TERM-38): `mem`, `mem32`, `gpio`, `bk`, `si`, `bt` on the command line (and `rx`, TERM-29, `gps`, TERM-30, `bk settle`, TERM-31, `bk turns`, WATCH-17.1, `mic`, TERM-32, `txtest`, TERM-33 and TERM-34, and `gps query`/`gps nmea`/`gps standby`, TERM-36), hex addresses, registers and values, answers on the console; Esc stops one, and a request not answered in 2 s ends it. They are in every build. Memory is read by the UI task where the terminal runs; BK4829 and SI4732 requests go to the Radio task, which owns the chips, one at a time.
> - **TERM-22** *(bench builds only, TERM-38)* `mem ADDR [LEN]` reads up to 1024 bytes of flash (0x08000000, 1 MB), RAM (0x20000000, 96 KB) or the information block: the boot memory (0x1FFFB000, 16 KB), the flash size and unique ID (0x1FFFF7E0) and the user system data (0x1FFFF800, 48 B: the AT32's option bytes, where STM32F1 has its own), shown 16 to a line with their characters; `mem32 ADDR [WORDS]` reads up to 256 aligned 32-bit words, and is the only way into the peripheral blocks (0x4000xxxx-0x4002xxxx, the reference manual's list of the blocks this MCU has) and the Cortex-M system control space. Anything else, a reserved hole between them too, is refused before anything is read, with a message listing what can be read, so a typo can't fault the radio. Some peripheral reads have side effects (a UART's data register gives up its byte). `gpio` shows ports A to E, input and output bits 15 to 0.
> - **TERM-23** *(bench builds only, TERM-38)* `bk CHIP REG` reads a BK4829 register (chip 0 VHF, 1 UHF and HF; 0x00 to 0x7F), `bk CHIP REG VALUE` writes one and reads it back, `bk CHIP dump` reads all 128, eight to a line. Never written from the terminal, in any build, checked by both the terminal and the Radio task: REG_30 with PA Gain (bit 3) set, or with Tx DSP (bit 1) set while any PLL/VCO bit (7:4) is on (decision 317: the transmit DSP alone, with no synthesizer, makes no carrier), and REG_33/REG_34 (the chip's GPIO outputs, wiring unknown). The radio's own logic can rewrite a register afterwards (a tune, Battery Save).
> - **TERM-24** *(bench builds only, TERM-38)* `si BYTES... [reply N]` sends an SI4732 command (up to 8 bytes) and shows the reply (1 to 16 bytes, status first); `si prop P` and `si prop P V` get and set a property. The SI4732 answers only while FM broadcast or HF mode has it powered; otherwise nothing is sent and the answer is "the SI4732 is powered down: FM broadcast or HF mode powers it up".
> - **TERM-25** The terminal also runs over Bluetooth: Enter opens it there whatever the cable port setting says. While a PIN lock is on it asks for the radio's PIN first (LINK-30); then, or with no PIN lock, it works as on the cable. Android serial apps reach it; iOS can't with the stock module.
> - **TERM-26** No CHIRP CSV in the terminal: the web app converts CHIRP and RepeaterBook files and writes over the link, keeping a CSV parser out of the firmware; the terminal's dump stays Ferrite's own format. The monitor keeps no ring of recent activity (0.5 KB of RAM saved).
> - **TERM-27** *(bench builds only, TERM-38)* `bt TEXT` sends one query to the Bluetooth module (TEXT then CR LF on USART1) and shows what comes back within 500 ms; with Bluetooth off (0 9 2 1) the module is powered for the query and off again after. Only `AT`, `AT+HELP` and `AT+WORD?` are sent: anything with `=` or anything else is refused before a byte goes out, so the terminal can never change the module (decision 79). `bt stat` shows USART1's rate, whether the module is powered, and its bytes in and out, framing and overrun errors and dropped bytes since boot; `bt baud N` (9600, 19200, 38400, 57600 or 115200) restarts USART1 at N until the next power-on, the MCU's side only: nothing is sent to the module (decision 152). `bt stat` also shows Bluetooth's pacing (LINK-36) and the bytes a second it allows; `bt pace B G` sends bursts of B bytes (0 to 255; 0 sends back to back) G ms apart (0 to 1000) until the next power-on, in RAM only (decision 156).
> - **TERM-28** Cable log (0 9 9 5, bench builds only: tx-bench and bring-up; a user build has no menu item and prints only a fault's report, decision 344): plain lines on the cable at 115200: the boot report (every check, the SPI flash's ID, the store's state, the SSB patch), then, while it's on, every key and knob step, the receiver once a second, beeps, redraw times of 5 ms or more ("draw N ms, K KB to the panel, SCREEN, interrupts M ms, F MHz": the pixel bytes sent, in every build; M the part of N the Radio, DSP and input tasks ran in, F the core clock, DISP-29), the GPS (power, sentences and fix every 5 s, every sentence sent to the module, GPS-26), the SI4732's start-ups and HF levels, the battery every 10 s, and Bluetooth's UART every 5 s while bytes move ("bt: in N out N (+N/+N), framing N, overrun N, 9600 baud, module on"). Bluetooth's terminal and link sessions run while the log has the cable; the cable's wait for its first byte. Every build prints the boot report; after it the bring-up build always logs, the normal build only with Cable log on. The first byte from the PC ends the log and the cable goes to the terminal or link as 0 9 2 3 says. A fault's report (`*** FAULT` and the address) prints in every build.
    - **TERM-28.1** The log holds only the cable: APRS, SAME and the pager decoder run from power-on whether or not it is on, with no PC on the cable (until Oct 22, 2026 they waited for the cable's first byte, so a radio with Cable log on and no cable decoded nothing). Only the bring-up build, which mounts no store, holds them until that byte.
> - **TERM-29** *(bench builds only, TERM-38)* `rx both`: both band receive selects held on, a bench test (decision 114).
>     - **TERM-29.1** `rx both` holds both band receive selects on, VHF RX (PB0) and UHF RX (PE12) (SAFE-24); `rx one` goes back to the one select the receive path needs; `rx` says which is in force; `gpio` shows PB0 and PE12. RAM only: off at boot.
>     - **TERM-29.2** Refused, with the reason, while keyed, in HF mode, with the relay energised (an HF slot) or while the crossband repeater runs.
>     - **TERM-29.3** A bench test (decision 114): reception on each band with one select and with both, and whether the broadcast clicks stop with a VHF and a UHF slot watched.
>     - **TERM-29.4** `bk swap A` (B, C) receives that slot on the other band's chip, the fox-hunt measurement (Alex, Nov 4, 2026: a signal attenuated without taking the antenna off): the slot's frequency through the other chip's front end, its level read as usual (the card's S-meter, `bk 1 67`); `bk swap off` ends it, `bk swap` says which. RAM only, every slot retuned on a change. With `rx both` both bands' receive paths stay powered meanwhile.
> - **TERM-31** *(bench builds only, TERM-38)* `bk settle` shows the BK4829 settle waits in force (PWR-21); `bk settle N` sets both to N ms, `bk settle W S` the wake and sleep waits apart, 0 to 20 ms each (decimal), 0 being stock's (no wait); out of range or extra words are refused before anything is sent. A bench test (decision 119): the next wake or sleep uses the new values. RAM only: 2 and 1 ms at boot. `bk settle` alone only reads.
> - **TERM-30** `gps` shows the GPS module's last GGA sentence as it was received (from `$` to the checksum) and how many seconds ago, then its field 9 (the altitude, which NMEA says is above mean sea level) and field 11 (the geoid separation) as read, in metres, or "empty". It reads only: the GPS task keeps a copy of each GGA (88 B of RAM). With the module asleep between fixes (GPS-26) the copy is the last one before the sleep; with none since boot it says so.
> - **TERM-32** *(bench builds only, TERM-38)* `mic` reads PA0, the mic level tap VOX uses, 256 times at 8 kHz (32 ms) and shows the lowest, highest and mean reading (12-bit), how many times the readings cross their mean (with a hysteresis of an eighth of the swing) and the frequency that would be for raw audio, and the first 16 readings. A bench test: a steady tone at the mic tells raw audio (crossings at the tone's frequency) from a level or envelope (few or none), which settled that it can't: PA0 is a level (bench Oct 13, 2026; `archive/mic-audio-features.md`). It reads only; the Radio task waits through it.
>     - **TERM-32.1** *(bench builds only, TERM-38)* `af` is the same burst from PA2, the receive audio the DSP task captures (both BK4829s summed, and the SI4732's: LINK-13.7), shown as `af PA2: ...`. A bench test: with every receiver squelched it reads flat around mid-scale (about 1,250); a level that reaches 0 or 4,095 clips (BCR-7), and the line ends with how many of the 256 readings were within 4 counts of either end ("N at a rail"; decision 294). The DSP task counts the same while it captures (the decoders or the PC stream): the readback's `audio` line gives `clips` (readings at a rail since power-on) and `peak` (the loudest sample of the last second, DC removed, 0-32767). It reads only.
> - **TERM-33** *(bench builds only, TERM-38)* `txtest MODE [FILTERS] [bypass] [eq|eq8] [LEVEL]` arms a transmit test signal for the next PTT key-up, measured off the air (a bench test: decision 132; `data`, the level and the polarity from the first bench run, decision 133; `data` the default for `afsk` too, decision 135; `afsk-bk`, decision 134; `bypass`, the equalisers and the `fsk`, `gmsk` and `g3ruh` signals, decision 308).
>     - **TERM-33.1** `txtest` arms the signal for the next PTT key-up; it never keys by itself, and it is used up by that key-up (`txtest` alone shows what is armed, with its filters and level; `txtest off` disarms; RAM only, nothing armed at boot).
>     - **TERM-33.2** MODE `4fsk`: M17's symbols at 4800 a second (levels +3, +1, -1, -3, M17's dibit map; +3 set for +2400 Hz deviation: a DAC peak of 4920, measured in `data` on the black radio), root-raised-cosine shaped (roll-off 0.5, 8 symbols), 40 ms of +3/-3 first, then PRBS9; the DAC-to-air chain inverts (a positive DAC swing goes out as negative deviation), so the generator sends the symbols negated and +3 goes out as positive deviation, as M17 expects.
>     - **TERM-33.3** MODE `sweep`: a 1 kHz marker for 500 ms, then the 23 third-octave tones from 50 Hz to 8 kHz, 300 ms each with 50 ms of silence after each, at one level (a DAC peak of 4000).
>     - **TERM-33.4** MODE `afsk`: Bell 202 HDLC flags (0x7E) and nothing else, no callsign or frame, at the APRS beacon's level (APRS-14).
>     - **TERM-33.5** MODE `afsk-bk` (`txtest afsk-bk [LEVEL]`, no FILTERS word): the same flags from the keyed BK4829's own tone generator instead of DAC1, to compare the two on the air: after the key-up's plan the chip goes to tone transmit as stock sends any internal tone (REG_30 0x0200 on a wide channel or 0 on a narrow one, about 50 µs, then 0xC3FA: the mic ADC off, so the mic adds nothing), tone 1 starts on the space tone (REG_71 2200 Hz, REG_72 0, REG_70 0x8000 | gain << 8 with tone 2 off) and at each NRZI change REG_71 alone switches between 1200 and 2200 Hz (two changes a flag, at its first and last bit); the gain is 66 at 100 % (meant as about 3 kHz in wide FM; to be measured), LEVEL scaling it (rounded, 1 to 127); the changes are timed by TMR5 counting at 1.2 MHz (exactly 1000 ticks a bit), each one's time counted from the start so the bits never drift; TMR5's interrupt counts the change and wakes the Radio task, which alone writes the chip; at the release the timer stops and REG_70 goes to 0 (tones off), REG_30 staying at tone transmit until the unkey's REG_30 = 0, as the end tone leaves it; PC12 and DAC1 are never touched, and REG_2B, REG_19, REG_7D and REG_50 keep the key-up's voice values.
>     - **TERM-33.6** LEVEL is a percentage of the mode's nominal, 10 to 150 (default 100), applied to the generated signal (`afsk-bk`: to the tone gain).
>     - **TERM-33.7** The DAC modes each loop from the key-up until the release, made sample by sample at 48 kHz from DAC1 into the BK4829's mic input (PC12 high), as an APRS burst is. The real mic stays live in them (the DAC rides the mic input, so the chip can't mute one without the other): keep quiet near the radio.
>     - **TERM-33.8** The key-up is a data key-up in every other respect: every transmit check, REG_7D[5:0] = 0x34, the audio stage (PB8) on after the PA ramp, no end tone or CW ID; the slot's power; the emergency prompt still applies; Dry TX keeps the PA drive at 0.
>     - **TERM-33.9** FILTERS `data` (the default, as an APRS burst goes out) sets REG_2B[2:0] to exactly 100 (TX HPF300 off; pre-emphasis and LPF on).
>     - **TERM-33.10** With `data` the pre-emphasis cancels the injection's 6 dB/octave fall: measured flat within 1 dB from 250 Hz to 2.5 kHz.
>     - **TERM-33.11** `flat` sets REG_2B[2:0] = 111 (TX HPF300, LPF and pre-emphasis off: measured falling 6 dB/octave above about 150 Hz).
>     - **TERM-33.12** Both `data` and `flat` also turn off MIC AGC (REG_19[15] = 1) and the ALC (REG_7D[6] = 1), keep the TX mute (REG_50[15]) clear, and put all four registers back at the unkey; `voice` leaves the chip's voice settings.
>     - **TERM-33.13** A receive-only bench build refuses it; the receive and tx builds a user installs don't have it (TERM-38).
>     - **TERM-33.14** Measured off the air with `tools/sdrtx.py --eye`, `--sweep` or `--afsk` (an RTL-SDR; `--afsk` also gives the bit timing jitter of the tone switches against an ideal 1200 Bd grid, the phase step at the switches and the discriminator's spikes there against mid-bit, for `afsk` and `afsk-bk` alike; `--selftest` checks the analyses on synthetic signals; `--eye` still tries both polarities and says when it found the signal inverted).
>     - **TERM-33.15** The word `bypass` after FILTERS (not with `voice`; decision 308) also sets the keyed chip's "bypass all AF TX filters" (REG_47[0] = 1, over the key-up's 0x6142) and turns its TX soft limiter off (REG_2F[7:5] = 000, the receive de-emphasis gain in [13:8] kept), after the FILTERS set-up and before the key-up; both are put back at the unkey, before FILTERS' registers. Measured on the black radio (Oct 27, 2026): the 3.1 kHz wall of `data` is gone, leaving the injection's own curve (with the limiter off: +14.5 dB at 100 Hz, 0 at 1 kHz, -16.6 dB at 5 kHz, -30 dB at 8 kHz), and the 1 kHz tone 6.8 dB under `data`'s at the same DAC level (1385 against 3046 Hz).
>     - **TERM-33.16** The word `eq` or `eq8` after FILTERS (any order with `bypass`, once; not with `voice`; decisions 308, 310 and 311) runs the generated samples through a pre-equaliser at 48 kHz that undoes the bypass channel in magnitude and phase together, so with `bypass` every tone goes out at the deviation the plain `txtest sweep data` 1 kHz tone gets (3046 Hz at the sweep's level) and the group delay is flat: `eq` from 100 Hz to 5 kHz (95 taps), `eq8` from 100 Hz to 8 kHz (127 taps; audio to 8 kHz at 3 kHz deviation takes about 22 kHz of RF, a wide-channel set-up). The channel, identified from the transmitted signals against the off-air captures (`tools/bench-blade/txident.py`): the DAC injection's first-order low-pass at about 168 Hz (minimum phase: its phase falls to -90° above a few hundred Hz, 0.5 ms more group delay at 200 Hz than at 1 kHz), the board's coupling high-pass near 35 Hz (left in place), and the chip's linear-phase digital filter (-2.4 dB at 5 kHz, -11.8 dB at 8 kHz). Designed by `tools/bench-blade/txeq_design.py` (complex least squares, a delay allowed: 59 and 65 samples) with the equaliser itself falling 40 dB or more above its band; predicted through the channel: each tone -0.6 to 0 dB against the plain `data` 1 kHz tone, the group delay within 140 µs from 200 Hz up, and the eye of 9600 Bd G3RUH open (txdata.py's slicer: 0 errors, about 40 % opening at 55 dB over the floor). The level comes from REG_7D's mic sensitivity at its top (0x3F, 11 steps of 0.5 dB over the data key-up's 0x34; measured linear under the bypass), for `eq8` also REG_40's deviation field at 0x57A (from 0x50A, the top four bits kept: +2 dB by the power law the tone generator's deviation follows, about REG_40^2.8), and the taps the rest (`eq` +1.35 dB, `eq8` -0.65 dB at 1 kHz); both registers put back at the unkey. At LEVEL 100 every test signal fits the DAC through `eq`; through `eq8` all but hard-edged `fsk 1200` (up to 89 %) and `fsk 2400` (81 %) and the sweep (29 %: its shape is measured there against the plain sweep at 29 %). Above that the filter's output saturates (clips, never wraps). The boost is digital, ahead of the DAC: the DAC's and the injection's noise floors aren't boosted by it.
>     - **TERM-33.17** MODE `fsk BAUD [rc]` sends PRBS9 bits as 2-level NRZ at BAUD (300 to 9600, any value, not only whole samples a bit), ±3 kHz deviation at 100 %; hard edges, or with `rc` (before FILTERS) each edge a half cosine one bit long, centred on the bit boundary. MODE `gmsk BAUD` sends the same bits through a Gaussian of BT 0.5 at h 0.5 (deviation BAUD / 4). MODE `g3ruh` is 9600 Bd packet's line signal: the PRBS9 bits through G3RUH's self-synchronising scrambler (x^17 + x^12 + 1: each bit sent is the data bit XOR the bits sent 12 and 17 before), `rc` edges, ±3 kHz. Like 4FSK the three are sent negated, so a 1 goes out as positive deviation. Their amplitudes take the sweep's 1 kHz tone (a DAC peak of 4000, about 3.0 kHz in `data`) as the scale; LEVEL trims them (150 % gives POCSAG's ±4.5 kHz). Measured off the air with `tools/bench-blade/txdata.py` on a bladeRF capture: FM-demodulated, each bit's samples averaged, the timing phase, polarity and PRBS9 offset found every 256 bits; the bit error rate (descrambled for `g3ruh`), with and without a running DC removal, the deviation at the bit centres and the eye's opening.
> - **TERM-34** *(bench builds only, TERM-38)* `txtest suite` arms every transmit baseband test for one PTT key-up, run as a fixed, deterministic sequence of steps (`ferrite_core::txsuite`) so that one SDR capture measures them all (`tools/sdrsuite.py`). It is armed, shown, used up and refused as TERM-33 (never keys by itself, every transmit check, a data key-up, a transmit bench build only: `tx-bench` (TERM-38); a receive-only bench build refuses it), takes no FILTERS or LEVEL word, and is refused while the TX timeout (TX-30) is under 90 s, the suite's 60 s with the backstop's margin. The Radio task runs it: from the key-up it writes each step's registers at the step's time (each counted from the key-up, so nothing drifts), changing only what differs from the step before, through the driver; it hands DAC1's parts to the DSP task, which keeps DAC1 running from the key-up to the release and switches generators between halves; the tone generator's flags are timed by TMR5 as `afsk-bk`'s. At the key-up it keeps REG_2B, REG_19, REG_7D, REG_50, REG_40, REG_07 and REG_52 and turns the slot's sub-tone off (REG_51 0); after the last step it holds a 1 kHz tone from DAC1 in `data` until the release; at the release (or any unkey) it stops TMR5 and DAC1, turns the tone generator off and puts every one of those registers back and the key-up's sub-tone again, before the unkey's steps. Each step sets what modulates (DAC1 on the mic path, REG_30 0xC1FE; the tone generator, 0xC3FA, through stock's REG_30 step; or the sub-tone alone on the mic path with DAC1 silent), the TX audio set-up (`voice`, `data` or `flat`, as TERM-33's) and REG_40's low 12 bits (the top four kept); every step outside section 4 has stock's 0x50A. Each section starts with a marker: 300 ms of silence (carrier only), then 150 ms of a tone from DAC1 in `data` at a DAC peak of 3000, at 350 + 50 n Hz for section n; the sequence starts with 1 s of silence and ends with 300 ms of it before the hold. The sections: (1, 400 Hz) the sweep's 23 third-octave tones from 50 Hz to 8 kHz, 250 ms each with 50 ms after, once, in `data` (6.96 s); (2, 450 Hz) the same in `voice` (6.96 s); (3, 500 Hz) DAC1's AFSK flags in `data`, at the APRS level (APRS-14) for 3 s, 200 ms of silence, then at 50 % for 2 s; (4, 550 Hz) the tone generator's AFSK flags (`afsk-bk` at 100 %) for 2 s at each REG_40 low value 0x300, 0x387, 0x3E0, 0x43C, 0x4A0 and 0x50A in `voice`, then 0x43C in `data` and in `flat`, each followed by 200 ms of carrier (17.6 s); (5, 600 Hz) the tone generator at stock's gain and REG_40, `voice`: 1000 Hz for 1 s, 300 ms of carrier, then each end tone of TONE-10 (Beep, Two-tone up, Two-tone down, Chirp, Quindar) with 300 ms of carrier after each (4.08 s); (6, 650 Hz) sub-tones alone in `voice`: CTCSS 67.0, 100.0, 151.4 and 250.3 Hz at the calibration's CTCSS gain (0xF210 byte 1) and DCS 023 normal at its DCS gain (byte 2), 2 s each with 400 ms of silence after (12.0 s); (7, 700 Hz) TERM-33's 4FSK in `data` at 60 % for 3 s, then the 300 ms before the hold. In all 59.95 s from the key-up to the hold. `txtest` alone while it runs shows the section and the time left; the cable log names each section as it starts. `tools/sdrsuite.py` finds the markers, measures each section (the sweep tables and -3 dB points of both set-ups; AFSK deviation, twist, bit timing jitter and phase; REG_40 to deviation with a fitted curve and the REG_40 for 3.0 kHz, the filters' effect and each burst's swell; the 1 kHz tone's attack and steady level; each end tone's frequency, length and level at its start and end; CTCSS frequency and deviation, DCS bit rate, deviation and code; 4FSK levels and symbol errors) and writes a text report and one HTML page with its charts next to the capture; `--selftest` runs it on the whole suite synthesised as IQ. A bench test (decision 139).

> - **TERM-53** *(bench builds only, TERM-38)* `bk spi` shows the BK4829 bus's read and write half SCK periods (Ferrite's 3.0 and 1.5 us, PWR-26.12; stock's 5 us for both, about 100 kHz); `bk spi use RD WR` sets them, 1.0-5.0 us in tenths, RAM only (`bk spi use 5 5` is stock's, for timing against). `bk spi HALF [ROUNDS]` runs ROUNDS rounds on each chip at HALF for reads and writes alike, the speeds put back as they were after (default 2000, at most 5000, the Radio task doing nothing else meanwhile): its ID read at HALF, and REG_48 written at HALF in four bit patterns in turn (its own value and that value XOR 0x5555, 0xAAAA and 0xFFFF; each pattern's expected value is what it reads back when written at stock's speed) and read again at stock's 5 us, so reads and writes are judged apart. REG_48 ends as it was; every slot is retuned after a test (a write at a failing speed may have hit another register). It says each chip's wrong reads and writes and the mean time of one operation. Measured on black, Nov 12, 2026, at 120 MHz: reads right at 2.3 us and above, every one wrong at 2.2 and below (a cliff: the turnaround held at 10 us changed nothing, so it is the data line's own delay, about 2.25 us); writes right at every half period down to 1.0 us. Green (BJ9000), Nov 12, 2026, with the four patterns: the same edge a little softer: reads right at 2.3 us and above, 2.2 us passing some runs and 78-94% wrong in others, every one wrong at 2.1 and below; writes right down to 1.0 us. Clear, the same day: reads right at 2.2 us and above, every one wrong at 2.1 and below; writes right down to 1.0 us. Stock's 5 us: 270 us an operation; 3 us 169; 2.3 us 134. Alex, Nov 12, 2026: the retunes' register writes (about 4.5 ms each) measured before the bus is made faster.
