## 25. APRS

### What it's for

Sends your position and messages over APRS and shows who's around: a heard list, station details, messages with acks, beacons and an optional digipeater.

### Using it

1. Set your callsign (MENU 0 8 6 1). Nothing sends without one.
2. **APRS mode:** MENU 0 5 3. Slot C becomes the APRS slot: it tunes to the APRS frequency, is watched, and decodes in the background while you listen on A or B; its card on home shows what's heard. With C the active slot, **PTT A** opens a message to send. Turning APRS mode off puts slot C back as it was.
3. **Heard:** MENU 0 5 1. Newest first; ▲▼ move, OK opens the station, # writes it a message, hold MENU opens the APRS list (APRS-17.7). There is no frequency Go.
4. **Station:** position, course, path, comment, last message, distance and a bearing arrow. OK writes a message, # saves it as a waypoint, hold MENU saves it as a contact (APRS-41).
5. **Messages:** MENU 0 5 2. Inbox, sent (with ack state), compose (recipient, text: predictive entry and quick messages, TEXT-1 to TEXT-11). An empty recipient lists your contacts, recent conversations, stations heard and APRS services: the knob picks, ◀▶ jump a group, OK takes one (APRS-38). Hold MENU there: write a message, quick messages (edit the nine, TEXT-7), contacts (APRS-40).
6. **The APRS list:** one list from the APRS card (OK, or hold MENU) and from the heard list (hold MENU): 1 Messages, 2 Heard, 3 Sent, 4 Beacon now, 5 Event log, 6 Clear the heard list, 7 APRS settings. A digit opens a list or screen at once, so OK 1 on the APRS card reads your messages; Beacon now and the others wait for OK. MENU 0 5 6 sets automatic beacons, their power and the values in their comment (APRS-54).
7. **Sent:** OK 3 on the APRS card. Your beacons since power-on, newest first: how long ago each went out and the digipeaters heard repeating it, or "not heard back" (APRS-53).

Per channel: **APRS** flag (decode APRS while on this channel), default off.

> **Spec: receive**
>
> - **APRS-1** APRS decodes from the receiver's own audio tap, whatever the speaker does.
>     - **APRS-1.1** Decode from the PA2 tap.
>     - **APRS-1.2** It decodes 1200-baud AFSK (Bell 202).
>     - **APRS-1.3** So decoding continues with the speaker muted.
> - **APRS-2** "Mute speaker for APRS" (0 5 9 6, default on) mutes C when APRS mode comes on and at power on; decoding goes on. \* on C while APRS mode is on flips this setting (and it is saved), and changing it in the menu mutes or unmutes C at once, so the two never disagree. Without APRS mode, \* on C is quick mute as on any slot (HOME-15) and leaves the setting alone.
> - **APRS-3** Heard list: newest first; callsign, symbol, distance, bearing, age, direct or via digipeaters. The symbol is named as APRS 1.2's tables name it, the alternate table's own meanings included (\\- a house, \\k an SUV, \\[ a wall cloud); an overlay takes the alternate symbol.
> - **APRS-4** The heard list is 50 stations, the oldest heard dropping out for a new one; each keeps its 40-character comment (APRS-23 reads weather from it) in its stored row and, for the stations heard most recently, in RAM (APRS-4.5). It survives a restart through its own flash region (`layout::HEARD`, 0x060000–0x06FFFF, 16 sectors: a ring of 496 rows of 128 B, 31 to a sector; decision 282, doubled by decision 333). A row is the station as listed (callsign, symbol, position, course and speed, altitude, the digipeater, comment, times heard, FX.25) with the time it was stored and whether that time was real (INBOX-6.1).
>     - **APRS-4.1** A station gets a row when it is heard and: it has none yet this power-on; or its position or comment changed and its last row is 10 minutes old or more; or its last row is 30 minutes old (a station still heard keeps a fresh time). The rule is `aprs_live::row_due`; the APRS task hands the row to the Storage task, which stamps it.
>     - **APRS-4.2** At power-on the list is rebuilt from the newest row of each station, newest first, at most 50, with their times heard. Their ages are unknown ("earlier", INBOX-6.1) until the clock is UTC again; then those stored with GPS time take their real age. A restored station heard again moves to the front as any other and gets its first row of this power-on.
>     - **APRS-4.3** Clearing the heard list (the APRS list's 6) erases the rows too; the reset (BOOT-14) and a first-boot format erase the region.
>     - **APRS-4.4** Wear, a busy case: 100 stations an hour, half of them mobile with a new position every beacon, is at most 50 × 6 + 50 × 2 = 400 rows an hour, 13 sector erases an hour over 16 sectors: about 20 erases a sector a day, some 14 years of the flash's 100,000 cycles on all day every day. A typical evening (30 stations, half mobile: 120 rows an hour) is about 6 a sector a day. The ring holds the last 496 rows: in the busy case about 74 minutes of stations.
>     - **APRS-4.5** RAM keeps, for each of the 50, only what the list and its counts need: the callsign, symbol, position, when last heard, times heard, its row's place in the ring and whether it was heard direct (about 40 bytes). The path, course and speed, altitude, comment and FX.25 note stay in RAM only for the 8 stations heard most recently; the others' come from their last stored row (one 128-byte SPI read when the station is shown or read over the link). A detail a station's frames haven't given since its last row is taken from that row, so nothing is lost; a row the ring has since overwritten leaves only the list's fields. Decision 287: about 3.3 KB less RAM than every station's full details.
> - **APRS-5** A station without a position shows "no position" and a dash for distance.
>     - **APRS-5.1** A position too long for one line on the station page breaks between latitude and longitude, never inside one.
>     - **APRS-5.2** A Mic-E report's device codes are not comment text: the type byte at the start (`` ` ``, `'`, `>`, `]`) and the model code at the end (`_3`, `[1`, `=`...) leave the comment, and the radio they name (the APRS device identification list, aprs-deviceid by OH7LZB, CC BY-SA 2.0) shows at the end of the station page's Comment row ("146.520 · APRSdroid", or "Yaesu FT5D" alone). An unknown model code stays as text. The heard row keeps the model (byte 92). The row's check covers its body, so the heard list stored before this build was dropped once at the update (found on clear, Oct 27: 9 stations gone). Alex, Oct 26: "those weird text chars".
> - **APRS-6** Messages to your callsign go to the inbox, play the message sound, and are acked (a message heard again: APRS-32).
>     - **APRS-6.1** The message sound is 0 7 5 5: Off, Ding (one note), Mail (a rising fifth), Packet (a short fake APRS burst: 1200 and 2200 Hz a bit or a few at a time, the default), Droid (a run of random chirps) or Pager (two chirps and a higher one), each under 0.6 s, from DAC1 with the amp on throughout (SND-11). It sounds over received audio as the alert tone does (SND-13), never while transmitting, and once per message (APRS-32). Each choice plays as it is picked. Only messages to you play it; other log events don't.
>     - **APRS-6.2** It has its own volume, 0 7 5 6: Low, Mid, High or Max (DAC levels 8 to 11, 6 dB apart; Max, the default, is the DAC's full swing). The volume knob sets the rest: the DAC joins the radio's audio before it (SND-2).
> - **APRS-44** FX.25 (Reed-Solomon forward error correction around an AX.25 frame) is received beside plain AX.25 whenever APRS decodes: all eleven codes (16, 32 or 64 check bytes), the tag found with up to 8 wrong bits. A block that corrects to a frame with a good FCS is heard like a plain frame (heard list, messages, acks, KISS); the same frame heard both ways counts once. The station screen's Path row adds "· FX.25, 3 fixed" (bytes corrected) while the station's last frame came as FX.25. The correction runs in the APRS task, never in the audio path: 3 to 13 ms of it per block.

> **Spec: transmit**
>
> - **APRS-7** Every APRS transmission goes through the Radio task's transmit sequence and chapter 15's checks.
> - **APRS-8** Refuse beacons, messages and digipeats until a callsign is set.
> - **APRS-9** At least 30 s between automatic transmissions, unless you press PTT. KISS frames are exempt (KISS-2).
> - **APRS-45** FX.25 on TX (0 5 9 5: Off, 16, 32 or 64 check bytes; 16 by default) wraps the frames the radio makes itself (beacons, messages, acks, query answers) in FX.25: the smallest block with that many check bytes, after the TX delay's flags. A frame too long for the code goes as plain AX.25. Digipeats and KISS frames go out as they came. Plain AX.25 receivers still decode them; FX.25 receivers repair up to half as many wrong bytes as there are check bytes. Kept in the APRS record, and in LINK-43's.
> - **APRS-46** The Messages options (hold MENU on 0 5 2) also give "Mark all read" (every message to you read, and its log entry, INBOX-7.1; the mail badge empties) and "Delete all messages" (inbox and sent, unread ones too, after a confirm; none of yours is tried after it, APRS-13.1). Alex, Oct 22, 2026.
>     - **APRS-46.1** Delete message: hold MENU on any message (in the list or open), "Delete message" and OK again (MENU-23) deletes that one, either way: to you, unread too (the mail badge drops it), or yours in any state (one still being tried is tried no more first). The list closes up under the cursor; an open message goes back to the list. On flash it is one bit cleared in the record (no erase, decision 323): the list skips it from then on, after a power cycle too, until the ring writes over its slot.
> - **APRS-47** Your symbol (0 5 5 2) is one of ten from the primary table: person (the default, what beacons sent before it could be set), car, jeep, truck, van, RV, motorcycle, bicycle, house or boat. Beacons and position answers (APRS-33) send it.
> - **APRS-48** TX delay (0 5 9 3) is the flags before your own frames: 100–1000 ms in 50 ms steps, 300 ms by default. KISS frames keep the host's own TX delay (KISS-2).
> - **APRS-49** TX level (0 5 9 4) sets the deviation of your own frames: Low (about 2.5 kHz), Mid (3.0 kHz, the default, bench-measured) or High (about 3.5 kHz).
> - **APRS-50** Your comment (0 5 5 3) is up to 40 characters, sent at the end of every position report: beacons and position answers (APRS-33); with 0 5 6 4 on, the battery, temperature and power follow it (APRS-54). OK on the row opens the message editor (multi-tap or predictive, TEXT-1 – TEXT-12) on it, with a 40-character limit (a beep and "40 characters" past it). It is kept as it is typed; OK closes the editor ("Comment kept"), and an empty comment is none ("Comment cleared"; # on the row clears it too). The row shows the comment, its start when long, or Empty. A comment that starts like position data gets a space in front so receivers read it as a comment. After a course and speed or an altitude, a space separates the comment so the raw packet reads cleanly ("/A=000242 On the road"; Alex, Oct 27, 2026). Kept in the APRS record (a record from before it reads none); the terminal's `set 0 5 5 3` takes the text whole, None clears it.
> - **APRS-51** Ambiguity (0 5 5 6: Exact, 1, 2, 3 or 4 digits dropped; Exact by default) blanks the last digits of both latitude and longitude in your position reports, as the APRS spec's position ambiguity: 1 the hundredths of a minute (`4903.5 N`, about 185 m), 2 the tenths too (`4903.  N`, about 1.8 km), 3 the minutes' units (`490 .  N`, about 18 km), 4 the minutes (`49  .  N`, about 1 degree). Receivers place the station in the middle of the box left. Beacons and position answers use the uncompressed form, which carries it (the compressed form has no way to). Kept in the APRS record (a record from before it reads Exact).
> - **APRS-52** Mic-E (0 5 5 7: Off, or one of the seven standard Mic-E statuses: Off duty, En route, In service, Returning, Committed, Special, Priority; Off by default) sends beacons and position answers (APRS-33) in the APRS spec's Mic-E form (APRS 1.0.1 chapter 10) with that status, in place of the uncompressed report. Status reports, messages, acks and digipeats are unchanged. Emergency is not offered here (a feature of its own).
>   - **APRS-52.1** The latitude, the status's three message bits, north/south, the longitude's +100 offset and east/west go in the destination address in place of the tocall (APZFER); its SSID stays 0 and the path and our callsign and SSID are unchanged. The information field is `` ` `` (current GPS data), the longitude, speed (knots, up to 799) and course (360 north, 0 unknown, as when standing still), the symbol, the altitude as `xxx}` (metres) with a 3D fix, then the comment (APRS-50).
>   - **APRS-52.2** Ambiguity (APRS-51) blanks the latitude's last digits in the destination (the spec's L and Z), and the longitude's same digits are sent as zeros, so neither gives the exact position away. A position at 179.99999 degrees is sent as 179 59.99 (Mic-E has no 180).
>   - **APRS-52.3** A comment a receiver would misread goes out behind a space or two: one starting with `` ` `` or `'` (old Mic-E telemetry, or a device type), `>` or `]` (Kenwood types), or with an `xxx}` altitude in its first five characters when no altitude is sent.
>   - **APRS-52.4** The row steps Off to Priority, stopping at the ends; # sets it back to Off. Kept in the APRS record (a record from before it reads Off); the terminal's `set 0 5 5 7` takes the names (`set 0 5 5 7 en route`).
> - **APRS-10** Never transmit while you are transmitting or the channel is busy: a frame waits for the first clear moment on the APRS frequency (p-persistence on C's squelch, as KISS: a try every 100 ms slot at 64 in 256 while C reads clear) and is not sent if C stays busy 5 s ("Beacon not sent: channel busy", logged).
> - **APRS-10.1** Nothing goes out once you have turned it off. When APRS mode and beacons are both off (or Low Power Mode or KISS takes over), everything waiting or asked for is dropped: an ack owed, a digipeat waiting its turn, Beacon now, the beacon after voice; a frame heard meanwhile isn't queued to digipeat. A frame already waiting for a clear moment is checked again before it keys and dropped (only a beacon may go with beacons alone), and a data frame keys slot C only while APRS mode, KISS or beacons hold it, so it never goes out on C's own channel given back meanwhile. Turning the digipeater off (0 5 7 1) drops a frame waiting its 0 5 7 3 delay. Your own messages wait for APRS mode as before (LINK-45). Decision 284.
> - **APRS-11** Frame length is capped; the station ID goes out as the protocol requires.
> - **APRS-12** Beacons and the digipeater are off by default.
> - **APRS-13** Sent messages show their state: pending, acked, retrying (the try it is on: "try 2/5"), failed, rejected (APRS-34), cancelled (APRS-13.1). A message is tried 5 times, 30, 60, 120 and 240 s apart, then marked failed; an ack stops it at once. A text APRS can't carry (`|`, `~` or `{`, which compose doesn't type) is marked failed at once.
>     - **APRS-13.1** Stop sending: on one of your messages still being tried (pending or retrying), hold MENU (in the list or on the message) and the options start with "Stop sending": no more tries, and it stays in the list as "cancelled" (grey, told apart from failed). Ferrite Console's Messages page has the same (LINK-49). "Delete all messages" (APRS-46) stops every try too.
>     - **APRS-13.2** Send again: on one of yours that failed or was cancelled, the options start with "Send again": it goes as a new message with five new tries (the old one stays as it ended), refused as compose's Send is (no callsign, KISS holding C, a remote session that may not transmit, APRS-13.3).
>     - **APRS-13.3** Four of your messages are tried at once. A fifth is refused before it is saved ("Not sent" / "4 messages sending"), so none is left pending for ever; the draft stays to send later.
>     - **APRS-13.4** A message is sent as one callsign throughout, since its ack comes back to that one: a callsign or SSID change while it is being tried ends it as failed.
>     - **APRS-13.5** Your messages still being tried at power-off end as failed at power-on (their tries live in RAM), so nothing stale goes out by itself; Send again sends one on purpose.
> - **APRS-14** The APRS burst's AFSK: from DAC1 into the BK4829 at about 3 kHz deviation, with the `data` TX set-up (decisions 133 and 135).
>     - **APRS-14.1** AFSK goes from DAC1 into the BK4829 mic input with the TX delay before the first flag.
>     - **APRS-14.2** At a level that makes about 3 kHz deviation, APRS's usual: a DAC peak of 3900 of 32767 (about 0.39 V peak to peak); a deviation calibration, measured on the bench: 3.0 kHz, twist -0.2 dB.
>     - **APRS-14.3** The burst goes out with the `data` TX audio set-up: REG_2B[2:0] = 100 (TX HPF off; pre-emphasis and LPF on), MIC AGC off (REG_19[15] = 1) and the ALC off (REG_7D[6] = 1) on top of the data key-up's REG_7D[5:0] = 0x34, the TX mute (REG_50[15]) kept clear; all four are saved at the key-up and put back at the unkey, as `txtest`'s `data` (TERM-33).

> **Spec: APRS mode**
>
> - **APRS-15** APRS mode (MENU 0 5 3, off by default) takes slot C: C's VFO or channel and its settings are kept aside, and C tunes to the APRS frequency (0 5 4; the region's default: US, Canada and Other 144.390, UK and Europe 144.800, Australia 145.175, New Zealand 144.575, Japan 144.640 MHz). Turning it off puts C back exactly as it was.
>     - **APRS-15.1** The way back to the region's frequency: # on 0 5 4 (MENU-18), or # in its entry box before typing (hint "# Region default").
>     - **APRS-15.2** APRS mode mutes C as it comes on, and at power on while it is on, when Mute speaker (APRS-2) is on: packets are decoded, not heard (APRS-21.1). \* unmutes it and turns Mute speaker off (APRS-2); turning APRS mode off unmutes it. The card shows it by the power (APRS-17.6).
> - **APRS-16** While APRS mode is on, C is watched whatever the Watch setting says, and the APRS decoder runs on it as its background job (WATCH-13); decoding goes on with the speaker muted when "Mute speaker for APRS" is on.
> - **APRS-17** Home shows C, while it is the APRS slot, as the APRS card.
>     - **APRS-17.1** Its header names what holds C, in this precedence: "APRS Mode", "APRS KISS" or "APRS Beacon", bold in C's colour, then the band (" · VHF", left out while APRS-21's priority stands beside the state, as that names it). The header's right is every card's state (HOME-4: nothing while idle on the large card, the RX tag, "Muted" or "RX muted" in amber); beside it, while C shares the VHF chip with A or B (APRS-21), who wins it (0 5 9 7, WATCH-17.3): "APRS first" in grey or "A·B first" in amber. Where the holder, the priority and the state don't all fit ("RX muted"), the priority hides and the state stays. A small card idle shows the priority in idle's place. Hold MENU on it opens the APRS list (APRS-17.7) as OK does (APRS-17.3).
>     - **APRS-17.2** The large card (decision 313, mock-up M3): the frequency at the compact cards' size, and right of it the counts (decision 282, option C): the stations heard since this power-on in bold ("3 heard", stations restored from flash by APRS-4.2 never count) and those heard in the last hour by real age (" · 11 /hr", a restored one counts once GPS time dates it, one of unknown age never); where both don't fit beside the frequency ("99 heard · 99 /hr") the " /hr" part goes. Under it two station rows (APRS-17.2.2), our last beacon's fate (APRS-17.2.3) and the beacon row (APRS-17.2.1) with the beacon power at its right ("LOW"). KISS: its port ("Bluetooth" or "Cable") right of the frequency, the frames sent from its client and heard since power-on as the first row ("Sent 3 · Heard 14"), "The app sends" on the beacon row (KISS-10). The small card: right of the frequency, bold, the heard count only, fixed (nothing rotates); with KISS its port; with beacons alone a two-line countdown, a small "Beacon in" over the time to the next timed beacon in C's colour ("5m", APRS-17.2.1), or briefly why there is none ("no fix", "waiting" for the relay, "by GPS" for distance and smart beacons, "soon" before the first). Its bottom line: with APRS mode the last station heard since this power-on and how long ago ("Last VE7ABC-9 · 2 min", or "Nothing heard yet"); with KISS the frames ("Sent 3 · Heard 14"); with beacons alone the interval and the position's source ("Every 10 min · GPS fix", "no fix" or "fixed pos"); the beacon power at its right. A beacon waiting for the relay (APRS-20) takes the small card's bottom line and the large card's beacon row in amber. The readback carries both counts (`heard=`, `hour=`).
>     - **APRS-17.2.1** The beacon's countdown reads "Beacon in 5m" (whole minutes, rounded up), then seconds in the last minute ("Beacon in 42s"); else the beacon state ("Beacon off", "Beacon manual", "Smart beacon", "Beacon soon", "Beacon: no fix" or "Beacon waiting: HF" in amber). The large card's beacon row says it with beacons beside APRS mode as with beacons alone (the header no longer says so, APRS-17.5). With beacons on beside APRS mode or KISS, the small card's bottom line shows the countdown at its right in place of the power ("Beacon in 5m" in violet, or why there is none in amber: "no fix", "waiting"); the last-heard line beside it drops "Last " when it would not fit. Nothing on the card rotates.
>     - **APRS-17.2.2** The large card's two station rows: the two newest stations heard since this power-on, newest first: the call (the newest bold, the second grey), how long ago ("13 s", "1 min"), and at the right the distance and bearing from our position ("2.4 km NE", in 0 6 4's units) when both positions are known, else "via digi" for a station heard through a digipeater, else nothing. With none, "Nothing heard yet" (or APRS-17.10's "Nothing since power-on").
>     - **APRS-17.2.3** Under them, our last beacon this power-on (the Sent page's newest, APRS-53): "Last beacon digi'd by VE7RHS" (the first digipeater heard repeating it; "Last " left out for a call of 9 characters, so the call is never cut), "Last beacon 12 s ago" while it is under 30 s old and not yet heard back, then "Last beacon not heard back" in amber. Before any beacon this power-on, a third station row (beacons alone: the interval and the position's source), or nothing.
>     - **APRS-17.2.4** While APRS messages are unread, the second station row becomes an envelope and "2 new from VE3KPW-9" (the count and the newest one's sender) in C's colour; read, the second station comes back. The readback's `unread=` and `from=`.
>     - **APRS-17.2.5** Without a position of our own (no GPS fix, no fixed position), the station rows show ages only (no distance column), and the beacon row says "no fix" in amber left of the power (with beacons on, the row itself says "Beacon: no fix"), so missing distances read as no GPS. The readback's `nofix=1`.
>     - **APRS-17.10** With APRS mode, when nothing has been heard since this power-on but the heard list holds stations from before it (APRS-4.2), the bottom line says "Nothing since power-on"; "Nothing heard yet" only while the list is empty (Alex, Oct 27: the card read "Nothing heard yet" beside 9 stations in the list).
>     - **APRS-17.3** With C the active slot, OK (MENU) or hold MENU on home opens the APRS list (APRS-17.7) on the row the hint bar names, and one more OK runs it: "OK Heard" (the heard list) with APRS mode or KISS, "OK Send" (Beacon now) with beacons alone (decision 313: "OK Beacon now" ran into "Hold MENU APRS"; the bar's three pairs keep 8 px between them). An exception to MENU-1 on the APRS slot, whose quick-settings rows are fixed anyway (APRS-19); 0 on the page is still the full menu (MENU-25), so MENU 0 paths work from C as from any slot. The hint bar: ABC Next, that OK, then "# Pause" while a watched slot is receiving (WATCH-6), else "Hold MENU APRS".
>     - **APRS-17.4** The readback's card line (TERM-37) for the APRS card adds `holder=mode|kiss|beacon`, `power=` and, while automatic beacons are on, `next=` (the countdown m:ss or its reason); with KISS `port=`, `sent=` and `frames=`, with beacons alone `line=` (the bottom line). >     - **APRS-21.1** In APRS mode, APRS is prioritized: while APRS mode or KISS holds C, C has receive priority on its chip. Its turns are 100 ms, counted only while its chip has the receive path, with the squelch's open delay at 0 (SQL-17), so a packet's carrier reads open within one turn: a packet starting in another slot's turn is caught within its 300 ms of flags (within 0.2 s with A sharing chip 0, 0.26 s with B watched on chip 1 too). Once C's squelch opens, C keeps the chip and the receive path until it closes (the packet's end), muted or not (a muted C is decoded, not heard), with no hold after. Another slot's 2 s hold after its squelch closes (WATCH-17) doesn't keep C from its turns, and a slot open on the APRS frequency itself hands the carrier to C. A slot already open on another frequency (a voice call on A) keeps the chip, so a packet meanwhile is missed, and a voice call that starts on A during a packet is missed until the packet ends: the accepted cost. The speaker follows the usual rules (WATCH-3): the packet plays only if C is heard. Beacons alone (APRS-29) give no priority.

> - **APRS-55** The APRS slot's receive data set-up (decision 309): every FM tune of a chip for the APRS slot (C while APRS mode, KISS or beacons hold it) turns off the BK4829's receive 300 Hz high-pass and de-emphasis (REG_2B 0x0500; the 3 kHz low-pass stays on), its receive noise gate (REG_28 bit 8, the register's other bits kept) and its AFC (REG_73 bit 4 set, the others kept), as stock's flat mode does. Tuning the chip for any other slot (a voice slot on a watch hop, the band scope, HF) puts the voice set-up back: REG_2B 0x0000, the noise gate and AFC on. It holds through retunes, watch hops, Battery Save's wakes (written again after a sleep) and the unkey's retune; a slot that becomes or stops being the APRS slot retunes at once. A key-up's REG_2B save and restore (APRS-14, TERM-33) is untouched, and the bench's `bk rxaf` override (TERM-42) wins while it is set. The decoder is the only listener: the packet audio a heard C plays (APRS-21.1) sounds a little brighter.
>     - **APRS-55.1** Why these bits: on the bench (decision 305) the 300 Hz high-pass and the noise gate cost data modes the most, and AFC can follow a data signal's slow content. De-emphasis was decided on recorded audio (`ferrite-app/tests/rxaf_study.rs`, the radio's own path at 24 kHz, the chip's de-emphasis as measured): with it off the decoder got 1,011 and 1,013 frames on the WA8LMF CD's two tracks (1,012 and 1,007 with it on) and 429 and 401 on the two off-air recordings (411 and 394). > - **APRS-56** Packets heard but not decoded (decision 355), for finding why a packet was missed.
>     - **APRS-56.1** The packet decoder counts since power-on: frames decoded; packets missed, a frame between two flags (aligned, at least 17 bytes) whose FCS failed on every slicer that framed it and that repair couldn't fix, with no slicer decoding a frame within 32 bits of it, and an AX.25 address field (2 to 10 callsigns); noise, the same without an address field (chance flags in noise); FX.25 blocks not corrected; frames dropped (the queue to the APRS task full). The UI readback's `decode` line shows them once any of the first four is above 0, in every build.
>     - **APRS-56.2** Decode log (0 5 9 8, Off by default): each missed packet and each FX.25 block not corrected goes to the event log, already read (no badge: a diagnosis, not news). The name is the sender's best guess, a `?` for each character a bit error spoiled; the text "Not decoded: 84 B, peak 35%, >APRS,WIDE1-1": the length with the FCS, the audio's peak over the last 64-128 ms as a share of full scale, ", FX.25" when a tag came with it, then the destination and path. A frame with no address field to read is counted as noise (APRS-56.1) and not logged (decision 366): on clear such frames were nearly all chance flags in noise, about 300 a day in bursts.
>     - **APRS-56.3** What it can't see: a packet too weak for its flags to come through never reaches the deframer. The APRS squelch's opens (SQL-19.4) against frames and misses tell those apart.
>     - **APRS-56.4** Bench builds only (decision 363; a tx build's `decode` line carries APRS-56.1's counts alone): the receive path's stages, counted since power-on on the same `decode` line: the AFSK captures started (`captures`, one per APRS squelch open that found the capture stopped) and the times the demodulator heard 1200-baud data (`heard`, each start of its carrier detect), and the capture's lost blocks (`late`: halves of the ADC's buffer the DSP task took after the next one was already written, 8 ms of audio each (decision 358)). With the squelch's opens (`aprs_sq`, SQL-19.4), frames and misses, a lost packet shows where the path dropped it: no squelch open (the chip wasn't on the frequency or didn't open), an open with no capture, a capture that heard no data, data heard but never framed, or audio lost under it (`late` moved). The same line times the capture's budget: the longest wait for and work on a half (`wake_max_us`, `work_max_us`), and the longest run while capturing of the executors that could hold the DSP task off (`radio_max_us`, `input_max_us`) with their runs over 1.5 ms (`long_runs`). The DSP task runs above the Radio task (decision 357), so `late` stays 0. Then the last capture's timeline: the APRS squelch's lag from its first open reading to the slot opening and the Radio task's tick then (`sq_lag_ms`, `tick_ms`), and the time from the capture starting to the demodulator first hearing data (`first_data_ms`).
>     - **APRS-56.5** The decoder hears the APRS slot's chip from its first open reading (decision 359): the capture starts and the chip's audio goes to the decoder (not the speaker) then, a tick before the slot opens; the slot's squelch is read every 10 ms while a slot is in APRS mode. A packet needs its capture running about 5-10 ms before its last opening flag: with the chip's own delay, flag leads of 50 ms and up decode.

>     - **APRS-57.1** Reply to: `*` (or nothing) is everyone; text then `*` is every callsign starting so (`VE7*`, `VE3DTJ*` for every SSID); anything else is one callsign-SSID exactly (SSID 0 written or not; case doesn't matter). Typed with capitals, digits and `-`; \* types the wildcard.
>     - **APRS-57.2** No reply to ourselves on any SSID, to a service (APRS-39), to an ack, reject or query (APRS-33), or to a station replied to in the last 30 minutes: two radios replying to each other stop at one reply each. One reply waits at a time; a message from a second station during the delay gets none. No room for another of our messages (APRS-13.3) or no callsign (APRS-8): none is sent.
>     - **APRS-56.6** Each capture's DC removal starts from its first sample, not mid-scale (decision 361): a fresh capture has no settling step under a packet's flags.

### Settled details

- Address book (APRS-37 – APRS-43, decisions 226 and 232): saved names show in the picker, Contacts, Station, and the Heard and Messages rows; no terminal command (Console covers it).

- Digipeating: a packet heard again within 30 s isn't repeated, at most one digipeat every 5 s, only on the APRS frequency, exempt from the 30 s spacing of your own automatic transmissions; the busy check, timeout and duty limit still apply (decided Oct 7, 2026). nicFW documents only its path and alias settings.
- Heard list: OK opens the station, # writes it a message; there is no frequency Go (decided Oct 6, 2026).
- Messages heard again (APRS-32): the text is part of what makes a message the same, so a station that starts its numbers again after a restart isn't silenced; every copy is acked, as APRS 1.0.1 asks (a sender retries because it missed the ack). Bulletins are out of scope.
- Queries (APRS-33, APRS-35): answered only while automatic beacons are on, since a station that beacons announces itself already and one on manual or off stays quiet; one general answer per 5 minutes so a busy area can't make the radio chatter. Queries the radio can't answer meaningfully (messages waiting, objects) are left alone.
- Rejections (APRS-34): APRS 1.0.1 leaves `rej` to a station that can't take a message; Ferrite takes everything addressed to it, so it sends none.
- Message length: compose stops at 67 characters (TEXT-5), the link's `SEND` refuses more or `|`, `~`, `{`, and a received text longer than 67 is kept to its first 67.
